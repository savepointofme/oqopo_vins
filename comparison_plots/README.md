## Divergence criterion (truncation rule)

A trajectory sample is treated as "diverged" if **either**:

1. `max(|x|, |y|, |z|) > 500 m` (absolute-position threshold), **OR**
2. `||p[i] − p[i−1]||₂ > 50 m` (single-frame jump threshold — flagging numerical explosions and orientation flips).

The curve is drawn up to the **last good sample** and that sample is marked with a large `X`.  The legend label appends the reason (e.g. `truncated (|xyz|>500m)`).

Truth-frame time origin = first ground-truth sample, applied uniformly to all curves and the GPS-end vertical marker.

## What the plots show

- **fly1**: monocular VIO struggles regardless of GPS. All 3 runs eventually drift past the 500 m guard.  GPS-aided runs survive longer than mono baseline but still lose scale by mid-flight. The `robust_gps_rel` variant lasts the longest.
- **fly2**: tight σ=0.3 GPS-altitude run hugs truth in both altitude and XY for the whole flight; loose σ=2.0 collapses.  Opposite optimum from fly4 — sigma is not universal.
- **fly3**: cleanest separation. No-GPS drifts in altitude (under-shoots descent); GPS-aided σ=0.3 tracks truth closely.  The `pr20_fixed_abs` run dives below 0 m altitude after t≈500s due to a known regression in that build.
- **fly4**: Stage A best (R2f) tracks truth altitude almost perfectly and bounds XY drift.  The Stage A divergent (R1z) explodes around t≈100 s — the dedup-exposed instability when σ=0.3 zonly cannot keep up with between-update drift.
